Uncertainty in the XC Energy and Potential

This page describes how SCF tracks the uncertainty in the exchange-correlation (XC) energy, \(E^{XC}\), and the AO matrix of the XC potential, \(V^{XC}\), when they are evaluated by quadrature. It is part of the uncertainty-quantified SCF effort described in Designing QCUP-SCF, and extends it from Hartree-Fock to Kohn-Sham DFT.

The uncertainty has two kinds of sources. The quantities evaluated at the grid points, e.g., the density, are uncertain; how their uncertainty is estimated is described in Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid. The quadrature itself is also approximate, because the grid is incomplete. This page covers how both reach \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\), and how the second is estimated.

Assumption: Asymmetric Intervals

Throughout this page and Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid, the UQ type is assumed to be an asymmetric interval. It stores a lower bound, an upper bound, and a mean (the best estimate of the value), and the mean need not be the midpoint of the bounds. For a value \(x\), the bounds are \(x - \delta x^-\) and \(x + \delta x^+\), and the deviations \(\delta x^-, \delta x^+ \ge 0\) need not be equal.

The reason for this assumption is that the density can not be negative, so its lower bound must be pinned at zero whenever an error model’s estimate would go below it. An asymmetric interval allows this without changing the mean. A symmetric interval stores only the bounds: pinning \([\rho_i - r_i, \rho_i + r_i]\) at zero gives \([0, \rho_i + r_i]\), whose midpoint, \((\rho_i + r_i)/2\), is larger than \(\rho_i\), so the best estimate of the density would be biased upward. The error of a quadrature also tends to have a known direction, which an asymmetric interval can represent. sigma::Interval stores only the bounds, so this type does not exist yet.

Errors in the XC Energy and Potential

Let \(\vec{r}_i\) and \(w_i\) be the point and weight of the \(i\)-th of \(N_g\) grid points, \(\phi_{\mu i}\) the value of the \(\mu\)-th atomic orbital (AO) at \(\vec{r}_i\), and \(\rho_i\) the density at \(\vec{r}_i\). For an LDA with energy density \(f\) and \(v = \partial f / \partial \rho\), the XC energy and potential of the functional, \(\tilde{E}^{XC}\) and \(\tilde{V}^{XC}\), are:

\[\begin{split}\tilde{E}^{XC} \equiv&\int f\left[\rho(\vec{r})\right] d\vec{r}\\ \tilde{V}^{XC}_{\mu\nu} \equiv& \int v\left[\rho(\vec{r})\right] \phi_\mu(\vec{r}) \phi_\nu(\vec{r}) d\vec{r}.\end{split}\]

These are the exact integrals of the chosen (approximate) functional, not the exact XC energy and potential, which are unknown. In practice, the integrals are evaluated by quadrature. Let \(E^{Quad}\) and \(V^{Quad}\) be the values obtained by exactly applying a quadrature rule:

\[\begin{split}E^{Quad} =&\sum_i w_i f\left[\rho_i\right]\\ V^{Quad}_{\mu\nu} =& \sum_i w_i \phi_{\mu i} v\left[\rho_i\right] \phi_{\nu i}.\end{split}\]

Of note these equations assume:

  1. The inputs (e.g., \(\rho_i\)) are evaluated at the grid points and are assumed to be exact.

  2. The sums are over all grid points in the quadrature rule, i.e., no terms are screened out.

Subject to these assumptions, the errors in the quadratures are then:

\[\begin{split}\delta E^{Quad} =& E^{Quad} - \tilde{E}^{XC}\\ \delta V^{Quad}_{\mu\nu} =& V^{Quad}_{\mu\nu} - \tilde{V}^{XC}_{\mu\nu}.\end{split}\]

If the sums are screened, terms are dropped from them and we actually compute \(E^{Scr}\) and \(V^{Scr}\):

\[\begin{split}E^{Scr} =& \sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}} w_i f\left[\rho_i\right]\\ V^{Scr}_{\mu\nu} =& \sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}} w_i \phi_{\mu i} v\left[\rho_i\right] \phi_{\nu i},\end{split}\]

where \(\mathcal{K}\) is the set of grid points that are kept in the energy, and \(\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\) is the set of grid points that are kept for the AO pair \(\mu\nu\) in the potential. The latter allows for screening that drops individual AO pairs at a grid point (e.g., because \(\left|\phi_{\mu i}\phi_{\nu i}\right|\) is small), not only whole grid points. The screening errors relative to full quadrature are:

\[\begin{split}\delta E^{Scr} =& E^{Scr} - E^{Quad}\\ \delta V^{Scr}_{\mu\nu} =& V^{Scr}_{\mu\nu} - V^{Quad}_{\mu\nu}.\end{split}\]

Similarly, if the inputs are approximated we actually compute \(E^{Inputs}\) and \(V^{Inputs}\) and the errors from using approximate inputs relative to full quadrature are:

\[\begin{split}\delta E^{Inputs} =& E^{Inputs} - E^{Quad}\\ \delta V^{Inputs}_{\mu\nu} =& V^{Inputs}_{\mu\nu} - V^{Quad}_{\mu\nu}.\end{split}\]

Note that the total error will in general not simply be the sum of the three errors because of cross terms. For example, if both screening and approximate inputs are used, then screening out a term will also change the uncertainty from the input uncertainty to that term. Other errors, such as floating-point rounding, can be built up using the same framework, i.e., as the difference between the value computed with the error and the value computed without it.

Throughout this page, \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) denote the values that are actually computed, i.e., with whatever screening and approximate inputs are used, and \(\tilde{E}^{XC}\) and \(\tilde{V}^{XC}\) always denote the exact integrals, i.e., the reference values.

Statement of Need

QCUP-SCF attaches an uncertainty to every approximately computed quantity by storing it as a UQ type, and lets the uncertainty propagate through the rest of the calculation by doing arithmetic with those types. For the ERIs, an error model attaches the uncertainty, and the uncertainty in the Coulomb and exchange matrices, the Fock matrix, and the energy follows from the arithmetic.

As just motivated, the uncertainty in the computed \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) has three sources:

  1. Uncertainty in the inputs. \(\rho_i\) and \(\phi_{\mu i}\) (and, for GGAs and beyond, further quantities such as \(\nabla\rho_i\)) are uncertain. If they are UQ types, the XC modules evaluate the quadratures with UQ types, and the inputs’ uncertainty propagates through the arithmetic without further consideration. Error models for attaching the uncertainty to the inputs are described in Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid. Without UQ types, we would need an error model to attach the uncertainty to \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) directly.

  2. Screening of the grid. The quadrature sums are screened, so some terms are dropped. This is an error in the quadrature, but does not stem from the uncertainty in any of the inputs, so it needs an error model of its own, which attaches uncertainty to \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) directly.

  3. Incompleteness of the grid. Even with exact inputs and the full quadrature, the quadrature differs from the integral. This error is attached to \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) by its own error model.

Error models for the first source are described in Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid. The purpose of this page is to design the infrastructure and error models for the second and third error sources. For simplicity we will refer to the latter two uncertainty sources as the error of the grid.

Considerations

  1. Each error source is counted once

    • Uncertainty in the inputs is attached to the inputs; uncertainty from the grid is attached to \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\). The error models must not double-count either source of uncertainty.

  2. Same interface as the XC modules

    • The module for attaching uncertainty to \(E^{XC}\) and \(V^{XC}\) is a wrapper around an XC module. It has the same property types, so it can be leveraged wherever the XC module was leveraged.

  3. As additional approximations are made, the module must be extendible so that it can attach uncertainty for those sources as well.

  4. The error model is a choice

    • As with the ERIs, there are several ways to estimate the error from the grid. The module should facilitate choosing among them.

  5. The uncertain type is chosen in one place

    • As in Designing QCUP-SCF, only the modules that create uncertainty know the uncertain type, and they get it from a UQ Initializer submodule. Here, those are the error models.

  6. Ordinary DFT is unaffected

    • Uncertainty should be done in wrappers over ordinary DFT or rely on templated DFT kernels to propagate uncertainty.

    • Code meant for use with traditional DFT should not need to know about uncertainty propagation.

XC UQ Implementation

SCF adds two modules, UQ XC Energy and UQ XC Potential, that are respectively responsible for computing the XC energy and XC potential with uncertainty. They follow the same pattern, so this section only describes the potential module.

As part of this design we introduce a new property type Quadrature<T>. This PT will live in the SCF repo for now. T here will be the property type of the quantity that is being evaluated by quadrature, e.g., for the XC potential it is simde::aos_xc_e_aos. In addition to taking the inputs T needs, Quadrature<T> will also take the grid. SCF’s XC energy and potential modules will be updated accordingly: each keeps its "Integration grid" submodule, builds the grid with it, and passes the grid and its own inputs to a submodule satisfying Quadrature<T>, e.g., SVWN3 Potential Quadrature for SVWN3 Potential, which evaluates the quadrature on that grid.

The UQ XC Potential module satisfies the same property type as an XC potential module (i.e., simde::aos_xc_e_aos). Like the XC potential modules, it builds the grid with its own "Integration grid" submodule, so that the quadrature and the error model see the same grid. The initial implementation will focus on attaching the uncertainty caused by using an incomplete grid. The current control flow is as follows:

  • Unwrap the inputs and build the grid with the "Integration grid" submodule.

  • Pass the inputs and the grid to the "XC Potential" submodule, which satisfies Quadrature<T>.

  • Pass the inputs, the grid, and the result of the quadrature to a submodule that estimates the uncertainty from the incompleteness error and attaches the uncertainty.

  • Call submodules for other sources of uncertainty, if any (eventually screening). Module’s design dictates whether the uncertainty is attached to the results of the wrapped XC potential module or to the uncertainty from the incompleteness error.

  • Return the final UQ results.

XCUncertain<Quadrature<T>> will be the property type that estimates the uncertainty from the incompleteness of the grid. The template parameter T is the property type of the module being evaluated by quadrature, i.e., for the XC potential it is simde::aos_xc_e_aos. XCUncertain<U> will be a PT that lives in SCF that takes the inputs of U and the results of calling a module satisfying U. The Uncertain<V> property type in SimDE should eventually be updated to replace XCUncertain<U>, but is out of scope for this design. The result of calling a module satisfying XCUncertain<Quadrature<T>> will be the input results with uncertainties applied, i.e., a matrix of the same size as the XC potential matrix where each element is a UQ type object that uses the input result as the mean of the returned result. It is the module which satisfies XCUncertain<Quadrature<T>> that is responsible for calling UQ Initializer.

The QCUP-SCF driver will be in charge of swapping out the error models. We will add submodules to the QCUP-SCF driver for each source of uncertainty, e.g., a submodule satisfying XCUncertain<Quadrature<simde::aos_xc_e_aos>> will be added to QCUP-SCF and targeted at the UQ XC Potential’s submodule. Users can thus control the error models from the driver level.

Incompleteness Error Models

The strategies below estimate the incompleteness errors, \(\delta E^{Quad}\) and, element-wise, \(\delta V^{Quad}\). It is assumed that no screening occurs in the grid. The value from the ordinary grid is kept as the mean, and the estimate becomes the deviations.

Reference Grid

Evaluate the quadrature a second time on a finer grid, giving \(E^{Quad}_{\mathrm{ref}}\) (or \(V^{Quad}_{\mathrm{ref}}\)), and use the difference, \(d = E^{Quad}_{\mathrm{ref}} - E^{Quad}\), as an estimate of \(-\delta E^{Quad}\). The sign of \(d\) is taken as an estimate of the direction of the error, so the deviation on the side of the reference value is \(|d|\), and the deviation on the other side can be zero or a fraction of \(|d|\). This is a heuristic: refinement need not converge monotonically, so the finer grid can lie on the wrong side of the exact integral, and a one-sided deviation then excludes it. Making both deviations \(|d|\) avoids this at the cost of a wider interval. This mirrors the Analytic Error ERI error model and its benchmark ERIs. It is an estimate, not a bound, and costs one evaluation on the finer grid, including the density and AOs on that grid.

The strategy is implemented as an error model, Reference Grid Error (see XC UQ Implementation) that satisfies the XCUncertain<Quadrature<T>> property type. It has a submodule, Reference XC Module, which satisfies Quadrature<T>. The module gets the original grid as an input and thus should be able to determine a finer grid from it. This requires that the grid has identifiers for its radial and angular quadratures. The module then passes the inputs and the finer grid to the Reference XC Module submodule and uses the difference to construct the deviations. Finally, it converts the original result to the UQ type with its UQ Initializer submodule, keeping the original result as the mean, attaches the deviations, and returns that uncertain result, not the difference. This requires that the Reference Grid Error module also have a submodule for generating grids.

Grid Extrapolation

Evaluate on a sequence of grids of increasing size and estimate the remaining error from how quickly the values converge. This is more expensive than a single reference grid, but gives an estimate of the error of the finest grid rather than the difference between two grids. It is a tool for choosing the grid and for validating the reference-grid strategy. It could be implemented as a series of successive calls to the Reference Grid Error module, but it is not yet implemented.

Verified Cell Enclosure

Partition space into regions, \(\Omega_i\), of known volume, and let \(\rho\left(\Omega_i\right)\) be an enclosure of every value the density takes in \(\Omega_i\). Then

\[\tilde{E}^{XC} \in \sum_i \left|\Omega_i\right| f\left[\rho\left(\Omega_i\right)\right] + \int_{\text{outside}} f\left[\rho\left(\vec{r}\right)\right] d\vec{r},\]

evaluated with UQ types, where the last term covers space outside all of the regions and must be bounded separately, e.g., from a bound on the density far from the molecule. The enclosures \(\rho\left(\Omega_i\right)\) are computed by this error model itself, not taken from the inputs of Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid, which only carry the uncertainty of the values at the grid points; taking them from the inputs would count the variation of the density across each region twice. This converges more slowly than traditional quadrature and is thus expected to give a wider bound.

A Priori Estimates

The error of the radial and angular quadratures can be estimated from their known properties, e.g., the highest degree of spherical harmonic an angular grid integrates exactly, together with how quickly the integrand’s expansion in spherical harmonics decays around each atom. This is cheap, but needs analysis per quadrature, and is the least developed of the strategies.

Further Considerations

  • Rounding in the functional. Evaluating \(f\), \(v\), and the sums over grid points also rounds. This is only accounted for if the arithmetic of the UQ type rounds outward. It is expected to be small compared with the other sources.

  • Thresholds in the functionals. The XC modules need a policy for points whose uncertain density reaches below a density threshold, or zero. Pinning the lower bound of the density at zero keeps the density sound but does not bound \(\partial v/\partial\rho\). Dropping such points, as LibXC does, must itself be counted as an error, and is part of the error of the grid.

  • Uncertain grids. GridFromIntegratorXX can make grids with uncertain points and weights. Uncertain points make the inputs uncertain and are covered in Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid. Uncertain weights enter the sums directly, so their effect follows from the UQ arithmetic in the XC modules.

  • GGAs and beyond. The inputs grow (see Uncertainty in the XC Inputs on a Grid), but the split between the inputs and the error of the grid is unchanged.
